1. The problem we solve

Compliance is stuck in a paperwork paradigm. Organisations are held accountable against overlapping frameworks — ISO 27001, ISO 27701, GDPR, NIS 2, SOC 2, sector-specific rules — each demanding evidence that a control is in place, operating, and reviewed. In practice this evidence lives everywhere: spreadsheets in someone's OneDrive, an HR record in Odoo, an access review in Okta, a firewall log in a SIEM, a training certificate in an LMS, a signed policy PDF on a shared drive.

The traditional response is a compliance platform that becomes yet another document repository — you upload copies, tag them, hope they stay in sync with the systems of record. When the auditor arrives, someone spends weeks reconciling what's in the platform against what's actually in production. Everyone loses: the auditor gets stale evidence, the tenant duplicates data, and every audit begins with the same question — is what's in the platform still what's true?

The shift we're making

Regulators increasingly want to see a functioning program, not a library of paper. ArionComply is built for that reality. We are the ledger that describes your compliance program — the map to where evidence lives, the freshness clock, the ownership record, the auditor-readable provenance — while your evidence stays in the systems of record that already hold it.

What "compliance program ledger" means in practice

  • We track where each piece of evidence lives, how fresh it is, who owns it, and which control requirements it satisfies.
  • We do not re-implement your HR system, your IAM, your CMDB. Those systems remain authoritative; ArionComply cites them.
  • We surface gaps, upcoming reviews, cascade implications, cross-framework overlap — things a human reviewer would miss at scale.
  • We support tenants who prefer to hold evidence inside the product (typical for startups without system-of-record maturity) with the same freshness & provenance discipline.

Two coexisting evidence modes

Not every organisation looks the same. A five-person startup keeps most of their compliance evidence inside a tool because they don't yet have separate systems of record. A 500-person enterprise has authoritative data in Odoo, Okta, Workday, ServiceNow — asking them to re-upload copies is both wasteful and a source of divergence risk. ArionComply supports both, in the same tenant, at the same time, on a per-control basis:

Stored mode

Evidence authored + held in-product. Templates, forms, tabular evidence rows. The tenant fills in structured fields; the ledger records what was filled, when, and by whom. Typical for smaller organisations and for evidence that has no natural system of record (Statement of Applicability, ISMS scope, risk register).

Cited mode

Evidence lives in the source system; we track the citation. A URL to the record, a verification cadence, an attestation (verified_by at a specific time), a note on whether changes were detected between checks. The tenant owns the source system; we ensure the citation is fresh and the trail is auditor-defensible.

Principle

For cited evidence, the system exposes and tracks — the tenant attests. There are no silent machine attestations, no URL scrapers that auto-populate a verification log. Every verified_by is a real user at the moment of a real decision. This is not laziness; it is audit-defensibility. When the auditor asks "how do you know this was current on Tuesday?" the answer is a signed human attestation, not a robot's guess.

Per-must granularity

The unit of compliance work in ArionComply is not the control (too coarse — an auditor can't tell you which part fails) nor the document (too disconnected — a policy document may satisfy fragments of ten controls). It is the must-contain item: a specific, verifiable statement a control requires. For ISO 27001 A.5.15 Access control, the musts include: "access rights are reviewed at planned intervals", "a formal registration and de-registration process exists", "a record of that review is retained", and several more. Each must has a freshness clock, an owner, and evidence linked to it.

3. Design philosophy

  • Deterministic where it matters. Every compliance-load-bearing decision — is this control applicable? does this evidence satisfy this must? is this cascade implication in scope? — is computed by a deterministic engine, not by a language model. An LLM may draft the prose that explains the decision, but never the decision itself.
  • AI where it helps. Language models are excellent at reading dense policy documents to extract structured fields, at answering natural-language questions with citations, at drafting starter templates. They are used liberally for these tasks. Every LLM call is logged with input, output, latency, and cost.
  • Provenance for every write. A boolean column on the tenant's scoping profile does not just carry true or false — it carries a source marker: was this declared by the tenant, derived by the system from another declared fact, or still at schema default? Downstream engines behave differently based on the source, and the audit trail always shows who or what said what, when.
  • Fail-open for user-facing prose, fail-loud for audit-critical decisions. If a display humanisation function encounters an unknown enum value, it shows the raw slug rather than crashing the page. If the applicability engine encounters an unknown driving fact, it does not fire the rule — because silently marking a control as N/A on a wrong assumption is the exact opposite of the discipline an auditor is looking for.
  • Human-in-the-loop by design. The tenant is authoritative on their own state. Every engine-proposed change surfaces as a proposal that requires human confirmation before it hits the live posture. AI outputs are never applied silently.
  • APPEND-ONLY for LLM outputs. When the conversational surface generates a compliance answer, deterministic checks may append missing citations or verdict markers — but the LLM's prose is never rewritten. The audit trail shows what the model said and what the deterministic layer added, both preserved.
  • Reconstructable from storage, not from memory. Every compliance-load-bearing decision is stored with enough time-fidelity that a future query can reconstruct the state as of any past date. Point-in-time posture is a property of the storage layer, not a reporting feature bolted on later. The corollary: audit-log tables have UPDATE and DELETE explicitly revoked from the application role — enforced at the Postgres grant layer and locked in place by static regression tests. No historic query can land on data the application was allowed to silently mutate.

4. The system in layers

ArionComply is composed of six functional layers. Each layer has a well-defined input and output; each has its own storage and its own tests. The layers are not strict pipeline stages — they interact in both directions — but the mental separation matches the way we build and reason about the system.

Layered composition — intake at the top, surfaces at the bottom.

Layer 1 — Intake

Evidence arrives in ArionComply through several distinct paths, each with different provenance guarantees and human-review points.

Workbook (round-trip) .xlsx with a hidden _arion_meta sheet Yes Per-column bindings mean each row lands on a specific must-item. Same auto-approve confidence as templated documents.

Layer 2 — Catalog

The catalog is the immutable-per-release description of what frameworks require. It lives in two coordinated stores: a graph database holds the network of frameworks, controls, must-contain items, evidence requirements, and the typed edges between them; a relational database holds the flat lookup tables that make queries fast. The catalog is regenerated from source definitions and never mutated in production — customer boxes receive a pre-built snapshot at install time.

What's in the catalog today, by numbers:

  • 3 fully-curated frameworks (ISO 27001:2022, ISO 27701:2019, GDPR 2016/679)
  • 220+ controls, each with an EvidenceRequirement describing what "satisfied" means for that control
  • 800+ must-contain items and 200+ should-contain items across those controls
  • 500+ typed cross-framework edges (11 edge types) describing how a control in one framework relates to controls in another
  • 53 cascade event types with 260+ typed implication edges
  • 17 topic bundles that map named business workflows (identity lifecycle, incident response, records handling) to the relevant must-items across frameworks

Layer 3 — State

State is what makes a catalog installation into a live compliance program. Each tenant has:

  • Posture — the current compliance verdict for every in-scope control: Not-assessed, Non-conformity, Opportunity-for- improvement, or Comply.
  • Applicability — a per-control marker of whether the control is applicable or N/A for this tenant, with a human-readable reason.
  • Scoping facts — the tenant's business profile: do they process personal data, in which regions, in what role (controller / processor), with what sensitivity categories, at what size. Each fact carries a source marker: declared, derived, or still at schema default.
  • Findings — the individual pieces of evidence attached to must-items, each with an inference source (extracted, workbook, templated, cited, manual) and a review state.
  • Tenant profile — display-value placeholders that get substituted into downloaded template documents (CEO name, DPO name, registered address, approval dates).

Layer 4 — Assess

The assessment layer is where the compliance verdicts are computed. It is the most disciplined layer in the system: every function is deterministic, every input is auditable, every output is testable.

  • Fulfilment engine — for each control, evaluates whether the collected evidence satisfies the must-contain items and produces a verdict (NC / OFI / Comply). Runs on a per-must granularity so that partial satisfaction is representable and actionable.
  • Applicability derivation — reads the tenant's scoping facts and applies 11 rules to mark controls that structurally do not apply (a cloud-only tenant has no physical premises controls; a tenant with no EU data subjects has no GDPR obligations). Rules only fire on declared or derived facts, never on unanswered defaults.
  • Cascade engine — when an event happens (a supplier is reassessed, an incident is closed, a training campaign completes), the cascade engine walks the graph of implications and creates follow-up obligations on the downstream controls.
  • Critic verifiers — for LLM-extracted evidence, a downstream pass runs a strict grounding check: the claimed finding must be verbatim-supported by the source document. Findings that fail the grounding check are dropped before they reach human review.

Layer 5 — Surface

Everything the tenant actually interacts with — the dashboard, the conversational chat, the Get Started sidebar, the Evidence Package export, notifications, the Profile page for scoping facts and preferences. Every surface reads from state; none writes to it without going through the appropriate assessment or intake path.

Layer 6 — External API

For programmatic access, a scoped /api/external/v1 namespace exposes read + write endpoints under fine-grained scopes (external:query, external:posture:read, external:evidence:write, etc.). A Python SDK covers all endpoints with typed response models and an exception hierarchy. Every external call is rate-limited and audit-logged.

5. Frameworks catalog

ISO 27001:2022

Full curation. All 93 Annex A controls plus the 25 management-system clauses (4.x through 10.x). Each control has must-contain items derived from ISO 27002:2022 implementation guidance. Multi-leaf evidence specs distinguish policy documents, records, program reviews, and operational procedures for each control.

ISO 27701:2019

Full curation across all four PIMS control families (A.7.2–A.7.5 for controllers, B.8.2–B.8.5 for processors). Includes cross-standard bridges to the underlying ISO 27001 controls so that evidence collected against 27001 also contributes to 27701 where the edges say so.

GDPR 2016/679

Full curation across all six operational chapters (Principles, Rights, Controller/Processor duties, Transfers, DPO/codes/ certification, Supervisory-authority duties). DerivedSpec architecture handles articles whose satisfaction depends on satisfaction of other controls (e.g. Art.24 is satisfied when its 10 direct-evidence children are collectively satisfied).

Ready for more

The infrastructure for regional add-ons (LGPD, CCPA, APPI, PIPEDA) is in place: region-scoping columns exist, sector controlled-vocabulary matches NIS 2 Annexes I & II, applicability engine reads from a rules registry that accepts new rules without schema changes. Curation of each regulation's controls is the remaining work per new framework.

How a control is represented

Consider ISO 27001 A.5.15 Access control. In the catalog this is:

  1. A RequirementNode in the graph with node_id = ISO27001:2022:A.5.15, business description, cited-authority text, category tags.
  2. An EvidenceRequirement that names the multi-leaf shape: a policy leaf, a review-record leaf, a program-review leaf, and an operational-procedure leaf.
  3. A set of must-contain items on each leaf: for the review-record leaf, items like rev_sla_met (was the review completed within its 24-hour promise), rev_identity_pair (are identity revocations paired bi-directionally between A.5.16 and A.5.18), rev_residual_cleanup (were downstream shares and mailboxes cleaned up).
  4. Typed edges to related controls in ISO 27002 guidance, to the ISO 27701 PIMS extension controls that reuse this evidence, and to GDPR articles where the same evidence contributes.
  5. Cascade edges: when an identity_offboarded event fires, a follow-up on A.5.15 becomes expected (proof that the offboarding was reflected in the access review record within the SLA).

6. Scoping & applicability

Not every control applies to every tenant. A pure-cloud company has no physical premises controls; a business that never handles personal data has no GDPR articles; a data processor's evidence looks different from a data controller's. ArionComply captures scoping facts and derives applicability from them, so a tenant sees only the controls they are actually accountable against.

Applicability derivation is fact-driven and human-readable.

The provenance model

Every scoping fact carries a source marker in a JSON column. The values are:

  • declared — the tenant explicitly answered this question in the Profile.
  • derived — the system inferred this from another fact (a tenant registered in Czechia gets eu_data_subjects = true derived from country, until they explicitly override).
  • overridden — the tenant explicitly disagreed with a derivation and set their own answer.
  • (absent) — the column is still at schema default; the tenant has not been asked. Applicability rules do not fire on defaulted facts.
Design rule

Conservative-in-doubt. A tenant sees a control until they have explicitly said something that makes it inapplicable. Silence is not a decision. This is the opposite of the "we already turned it off for you" default that produces audit surprises when a tenant realises weeks later that a control they cared about was hidden.

Cascade of applicability

When the applicability engine marks a control N/A, three downstream systems respect the marking automatically:

  1. The conversational chat injects the N/A list into the LLM system prompt so the model does not recommend actions on inapplicable controls.
  2. The fulfilment engine skips scoring N/A controls entirely — they do not contribute to the tenant's Non-conformity count.
  3. The cascade engine refuses to fire implications on N/A targets — an incident lifecycle event does not create a follow-up on a physical-security control at a cloud-only tenant.

7. The conversational surface

The chat surface is where compliance-natural-language questions become answers: "what are our access control gaps?", "how do we remediate our NC on Art.32?", "what does A.5.15 require?". Behind the innocent phrasing is a substantial pipeline that combines deterministic retrieval, deterministic ranking, LLM prose generation, and deterministic post-processing.

Chat pipeline — the LLM answers, deterministic layers verify.

The seven signals

When a chat query arrives, seven signals evaluate in parallel and each contributes weighted votes to which control-refs the query is about:

  1. Retrieval (A) — semantic search over control texts.
  2. Explicit refs (B) — regex-match user-typed refs like A.5.15 or Art.32.1.b. Weight 1.00.
  3. Curated lexicon (C) — the human-authored map of business phrases to control refs. Highest weight (1.00) — when a curator says "chatgpt policy" maps to A.8.19, that mapping wins.
  4. Posture boost (D) — tenant NC/OFI relevance.
  5. Graph tightness (E) — controls in the same family cluster.
  6. Framework hint (F) — token match on "GDPR" / "ISO 27001".
  7. Session context (G) — deictic follow-ups ("what about that control?" resolves against the previous turn's cited refs).

Only if the deterministic signals fail to reach a confident verdict does the gatekeeper LLM arbiter get consulted. And even then, the LLM cannot invent — it can approve, modify, or reject candidates, but hard-locks prevent it from overriding a signal that fired cleanly.

The case-file digest

Once the target refs are known, the resolver assembles a case-file digest: a compact, structured evidence packet containing the tenant's posture on the target refs, the graph neighbourhood, the cited MUSTs, session context, and any scope-N/A annotations. This packet averages 650 tokens today, versus 21,731 tokens in the legacy path that was retired in Ship 2'.n. The result is dramatically lower LLM cost and higher answer quality — the model spends its attention budget on the relevant material, not on a wall of context.

The preservation check

After the LLM answers, a deterministic pass checks that every citation and verdict marker the digest promised is actually present in the answer prose. If the model dropped a required ref or a [DRAFT] tag (mandatory hedge for un-confirmed postures), the repair pass appends the missing elements as an audit footer: 

Access rights are reviewed quarterly (A.5.15 [NC-DRAFT] — register incomplete).

↳ Compliance facts: [NC-DRAFT] A.5.18 (access rights review overdue).
↳ Bridges to ISO 27001 for Art.32: A.5.15 [Comply], A.5.18 [NC-DRAFT].

The LLM's prose is never rewritten. The audit trail preserves both what the model said and what the deterministic layer added.

8. Cascade engine

Compliance is not a set of independent checkboxes. When a supplier is reassessed, several downstream obligations become newly relevant (contract review, risk register update, DPIA refresh if the supplier processes personal data). When an incident closes, obligations flow to lessons-learned, root-cause analysis, and evidence-of-improvement controls. The cascade engine models these implications explicitly.

Vocabulary

  • 53 event types — the things that happen (supplier assessed, incident triaged, training completed, access reviewed, contract renewed, threshold crossed).
  • 260+ typed edges — TRIGGERS_OBLIGATION, EXPECTS_FOLLOWUP_EVENT, BLOCKS_WHEN, DEMONSTRATES, IMPLEMENTS, SUPPORTS, ENABLES, GOVERNANCE — each with different walk semantics.
  • Meta-cascade patterns — aggregation thresholds (fire the "high incident rate" downstream event when 5+ minor incidents occur in a 30-day window), blocking conditions (skip this cascade when the target's applies_when predicate is false), policy overrides (tenant can mute specific cascade patterns).

What the tenant sees

Every fired cascade produces a follow-up: a dated obligation on a downstream control with a stated reason (which event fired, which edge was walked, when the follow-up is due). Follow-ups appear on the dashboard, generate notifications when they come near due date, and get resolved when the tenant attaches confirming evidence to the target control.

What the tenant does not see

Cascade fires that were suppressed. Every applicability-blocked cascade gets an info-log entry so an auditor asking "why didn't the training-overdue event flow to this policy?" gets a traceable answer — the target was marked N/A because the tenant declared they have no on-premises staff.

9. Audit trail & auditor deliverables

The most compelling artefact ArionComply produces is the one it hands to an external auditor. Everything upstream — the intake pipeline, the posture engine, the cascade implications, the human-review gates — exists so that this deliverable is defensible. Three interlocking capabilities make it so.

Integrity-verified audit trail

Log tables split into two disciplined shapes, enforced at the Postgres grant layer:

Compliance-load-bearing (9 tables)

INSERT + SELECT only. The application role cannot silently rewrite or erase these rows. Any correction goes through an explicit superuser flow with its own provenance capture.

  • posture_status_log — every posture change
  • applicability_status_log — every applicability rule fire
  • client_facts_log — user-driven fact changes
  • client_fact_change_log — cascade-driven fact changes
  • audit_log — system-wide who/what/old/new/ip
  • confirmation_log — tenant posture confirmations
  • deletion_log — deletion provenance
  • cascade_suppression_log — paths considered but skipped
  • external_evidence_verification_log — cite verifications

Diagnostic (7 tables)

INSERT + SELECT + DELETE, never UPDATE. These carry engineering telemetry, not compliance evidence. Retention sweeps may age them out; nobody may silently rewrite an entry.

  • ai_call_log — LLM cost/latency/prompt debug
  • chat_casefile_log — digest observability
  • chat_consensus_log — consensus tuning
  • fact_recompute_log — sweep observability
  • intake_trace_log — intake pipeline QA
  • intake_consensus_log — extraction consensus tuning
  • request_trace_log — chat routing metadata

A seventeenth table (audit_ledger_download_token) sits in a third narrow shape: INSERT + SELECT + UPDATE, never DELETE. UPDATE is needed for the times-used counter and revoke fields; DELETE is disallowed because token access logs are themselves auditor-defensibility evidence for how many times a specific auditor engagement fetched the ledger. Auditor access history must not disappear.

The intended grant shape for all 17 tables is locked by tests/test_audit_table_grants.py. A pre-commit and CI-runnable guard (scripts/ci/check_forbidden_patterns.sh) prevents future migrations from silently regressing the discipline: any new blanket GRANT ... ON ALL TABLES ... TO arioncomply_app, or any schema_v* file granting UPDATE/DELETE on a *_log or *_audit table, fails the commit unless it is deliberately annotated with an -- APPEND-ONLY-EXEMPT marker explaining why.

Point-in-time posture reconstruction

Any historic moment becomes a queryable state. The snapshot module takes an as_of date and reconstructs the tenant's posture as it stood on that date, from three already-time-tracked sources:

  • posture_assertions supersession trail — every finding change, with reason and source captured at write time.
  • document_findings lifecycle timestamps — evidence, with an expires_at guard so stale documents don't appear on historical snapshots as if they were current.
  • triggered_implication — cascade follow-ups that were open on that date.

The response includes a coverage_notes block reporting per-axis reconstruction fidelity. When a query lands before the corresponding audit table began tracking, the snapshot honestly reports "applicability: current-only — historical tracking begins 2026-09-05" rather than misrepresenting historical state as full-reconstruction.

Three response formats: JSON (for programmatic access), CSV (auditor spreadsheets), and print-optimised HTML with browser Save-as-PDF workflow — no server-side PDF dependency, better accessibility, URL-shareable. Endpoint: GET /api/v1/admin/posture-snapshot?as_of=YYYY-MM-DD&fmt=json|csv|html.

Design principle

Point-in-time is a property of the storage layer, not a feature bolted onto reporting. The raw material was always time-tracked (posture supersession trail, document lifecycle timestamps, cascade implications with fired-at/resolved-at). The gap was the query layer that composed them. When storage decisions are made with time-fidelity in mind up front, the reporting layer can be added later without data migrations.

Auditor's Ledger

The whole-program deliverable. When an external auditor arrives at a B2B customer, the tenant admin generates an Auditor's Ledger — a self-contained document that combines the point-in-time snapshot with cover-page provenance, per-framework tables, and a data-protection footer — and hands the auditor a one-time-download URL.

The ledger includes:

  • Cover page — tenant name, as-of date, generation timestamp, unique ledger_id, auditor firm + engagement reference, PII redaction level, verbatim-excerpts opt-in status.
  • Summary grid — 8 cards showing total controls, NC / OFI / Comply / N/A distribution, evidence-row count, open follow-ups.
  • Coverage notes — per-axis reconstruction fidelity (as in the snapshot).
  • Per-framework tables — one section per enrolled framework, control ref + verdict pill + reason, sorted by ref.
  • Data-protection footer + watermark — retention statement, "not a certification" disclaimer, watermark with ledger_id on every page.

Privacy discipline — data minimisation by default

The default posture is redact by default, disclose by explicit choice per generation. Every ledger generation captures its own choices; those choices are frozen at token creation and displayed on the cover page.

  • PII redaction always on. Email, US and international phone, SSN, credit card, IBAN, IPv4 addresses, Czech RČ, UK NINO, French NIR are all scrubbed at the default level. IPv6 is scrubbed at the strict level. Names are deliberately not redacted — compliance depends on named accountability.
  • User pseudonymisation always on. Reviewer and attester identifiers become user-XXXXXX with a per-tenant salt that the auditor cannot reverse (SHA-256 over arion-ledger-salt-v1:{tenant_id}, first 32 chars).
  • Verbatim source excerpts opt-in. When include_verbatim_excerpts is off (default), the ledger reports coverage counts and gap reasons; source-document text is not included. When on, per-control excerpts appear inline — but the ledger cover page states so explicitly.

One-time-download URL delivery

The auditor never gets an API key. The tenant admin generates a token via Profile → Auditor packages, and the resulting URL is what gets shared. The URL:

  • Has frozen generation parameters at token creation. If the auditor fetches multiple times (up to max_uses), the ledger regenerates with identical parameters. What the tenant agreed to disclose is a stable row, not a moving target.
  • Has a required expiry (default 7 days). No indefinite-lifetime tokens.
  • Is revocable at any time from the same Profile view. Revocation is instant.
  • Has every access attempt logged, not just successful downloads. Expired-token attempts, revoked-token attempts, and max-uses-reached attempts all land in the access log — a URL leak or retention violation becomes visible to the tenant.
Design principle

The token row IS the acknowledgement. It carries created_by, created_at, all generation parameters, label, auditor firm, and engagement reference — every field a "did the tenant knowingly release this?" question would need to answer. No separate acknowledgement table; no log-of-a-log indirection. The UI's job is to force the acknowledgement to be explicit and gated: a prominent panel with a checkbox that must be ticked before the generate button enables.

How the three layers compose

The integrity chain is end-to-end: the audit-log tables cannot be silently mutated, so every past state is faithful to what happened. The point-in-time snapshot reads that faithful trail and reconstructs the historical state, not an approximation. The Auditor's Ledger wraps that reconstruction with cover-page provenance and redaction, and delivers it through a revocable, logged, frozen-at-creation URL. Ask "what did you know on 14 March?" and the answer is a document the auditor can verify was not tampered with in transit or in generation.

10. Multi-tenant, multi-framework

ArionComply is designed for tenants who work against several frameworks simultaneously. A typical ISMS-first customer starts with ISO 27001, adds GDPR as an obligation the same organisation is accountable against, then layers ISO 27701 as a PIMS extension. The same evidence contributes to multiple frameworks through typed edges rather than duplicated uploads.

Row-level security by tenant

Every posture, finding, notification, and evidence record is tenant-scoped at the database layer via row-level security policies. The runtime application role has permission to read these tables but the security policy filters every query to the current tenant (established via a session-scoped tenant-id variable that the API sets from the caller's API key). A programmer error that forgets to pass tenant_id doesn't leak data — the security policy returns zero rows.

Framework role model

The three frameworks currently in the catalog are related by an explicit role model: ISO 27001 is a program framework; ISO 27701 is an extension that sits on top; GDPR is an obligation a tenant may be held accountable against. The framework role model matters for how the fulfilment engine attributes evidence and for how the chat presents cross-framework relationships. Adding NIS 2 to the catalog will make it an obligation for most tenants and a program for national CERTs.

11. Human–AI collaboration

This section is important because ArionComply is unusual in two ways: the AI component is bounded to specific roles by design, and the entire system was itself designed and built through human–AI collaboration. Both axes deserve explicit description.

Who does what at runtime

Human is authoritative on

  • Tenant posture (any change requires human confirmation)
  • Scoping-fact declarations (what the tenant IS)
  • Sector, region, role — all controlled-vocabulary picks
  • Template authorship and evidence content
  • Every Stage-1 review of extracted findings
  • Every Stage-2 review of engine-proposed verdicts
  • Every verified_by attestation on cited evidence
  • Curator lexicon and topic bundle definitions

AI drafts, ranks, extracts

  • Chat prose (bounded by digest + preservation check)
  • Field extraction from uploaded documents (verified verbatim)
  • Question-type classification (bounded arbiter, can't invent)
  • Enrichment of catalog metadata (curator reviews)
  • Consensus signal ranking (deterministic + LLM tiebreak)
  • Never — decide applicability, mutate posture silently, invent citations, produce a compliance verdict
The load-bearing rule

No compliance-load-bearing decision is made by an LLM without a deterministic gate. The engine decides applicability; the tenant confirms posture; the LLM drafts the explanation. This means every AI action has an auditable checkpoint — a rule that fired, a verifier that passed, a human that clicked approve — before it reaches production state.

Provenance for every LLM call

Every LLM call in the system is logged with purpose label, prompt, completion, latency, and cost. Every chat turn logs the case-file digest, the LLM response, and every deterministic repair event. Every consensus decision logs the seven signals' scores and the arbiter's verdict. This means operators can audit exactly what any model said, when, why, and what deterministic layer wrapped it — for any given turn, in production.

How this system itself was built

ArionComply is developed by a single human operator working with Claude (Anthropic's language model) as the design and implementation partner. This is not a marketing angle — it is a load-bearing fact about how the codebase looks and how it evolves. The collaboration has a specific rhythm:

  • Small arcs. A "ship" is a coherent piece of work, typically decomposed into a handful of sub-arcs delivered over hours to days. As of this document, the catalog is at Ship 116.
  • Every arc has a retrospective. Written to docs/memory/, indexed in MEMORY.md, and cross-linked from the codebase. The retrospective codifies lessons that constrain future arcs.
  • Every commit is co-authored. Commits are attributed to both the human operator and Claude, per Co-Authored-By markers in commit messages.
  • Every deployment is logged twice. The dev-side markdown log (committed to git) and the PoC-side JSONL log (appended by install.sh on the customer box) together give a persistent, machine-parseable history of what was deployed where and when.

Why this transparency matters

Compliance customers are entitled to know what parts of their compliance program were designed by a human, what parts by an AI, and how the two interact. In ArionComply, the answer is: the curated frameworks and the deterministic engines are human-authored; the extraction, chat, and drafting paths are AI-authored with human review at every load-bearing decision point. The entire codebase, including every AI-suggested change, went through the same human review that any production code goes through in any careful shop. This is an example of what "responsible AI in compliance" can look like: neither a rubber-stamp on AI outputs nor a rejection of AI's real capabilities, but a bounded collaboration with auditable checkpoints.

12. How we build

Ship arc discipline

Work is organised in "ship arcs" — cohesive units of change that ship as a group of commits with a shared retrospective. Sub-arcs (Ship 110'.a, 110'.b, ...) decompose an arc into shippable increments; a closer sub-arc (usually the last) writes the retrospective. The arc pattern reduces context switching, gives clear rollback boundaries, and produces a compact history future work can build on.

Retrospective discipline

Every arc closes with a retrospective in docs/memory/. The retrospective captures: what was delivered, what changed in the code base, what lessons were codified (numbered), what was deferred to future arcs, and what related arcs it depends on or supersedes. Codified lessons are referenced in future retrospectives — the sequence of lessons is itself the accumulated design memory of the project.

Testing discipline

  • Eval suite — 238 end-to-end evaluation cases covering the chat pipeline. Runs before every deploy. The baseline is 237 PASS + 1 known-stable WARN; any regression below 236 blocks the ship.
  • Unit tests for load-bearing helpers (country normalisation, sector validation, applicability rule shape, ID types, consensus signal weights, cross-framework edge invariants).
  • Grep guards for regression prevention. When we canonicalise a name or retire a pattern, we add a static grep guard that fails the build if the retired pattern reappears in runtime code.
  • Retrospective-as-test. Codified lessons in retros are themselves informal tests — future arcs check the lesson list before touching a related area.

Golden image discipline

Fresh installs of ArionComply do not replay every schema migration and re-embed every document from scratch. They load pre-built golden images (Postgres schema snapshot, curator seed data, graph export, embedding tarball). A pre-commit hook keeps the Postgres and graph goldens automatically fresh; the embedding golden is refreshed manually when embedding sources change (that cost is real: ~$2 OpenAI per rebuild). Fresh install completes in ~2 minutes instead of the 90 minutes the incremental path would require.

13. Deployment & operations

Provider-agnostic

ArionComply runs on any Ubuntu 24.04 host with 8+ GB RAM and 60+ GB disk. Azure VM, AWS EC2, GCP Compute Engine, on-prem VMware, bare-metal Intel NUC — the install script does not know or care. This matters for customers who prefer to keep compliance workloads on-premise: no cloud vendor lock-in is baked into the architecture.

Two operational patterns

Fresh install (once per box)

Interactive step 1 provisions secrets and writes the environment file. Step 2 runs the installer, which loads golden images across all three stores, wires system services, and starts the API. Two-step sequence, only the first needs a TTY.

bash scripts/ops/init-secrets.sh
bash deploy/install.sh

Per-arc update (every ship)

Every shipped arc has a dedicated scripts/ops/ship-N-poc-update.sh committed to git. Operator runs one SSH one-liner that pulls the latest code and invokes the arc's script. Migrations apply idempotently; API restarts; verification queries check the arc-specific delta.

ssh <host> '
  cd /data/arioncomply &&
  git pull &&
  bash scripts/ops/ship-N-poc-update.sh
'

Deployment tracking

Two complementary logs make deployment state persistent and machine-parseable:

  • Dev-side markdown timelines in docs/deployments/<host>.md, committed to git. Updated before ssh commands run (planned action) and after they succeed (flip to GREEN). Cross-links to arc retrospectives.
  • PoC-side JSONL log at /data/arioncomply/.deployment_log.jsonl, appended automatically by install.sh on every run. Machine-parseable with fields for git SHA, migrations applied, invoker, and outcome.

System-managed services

  • arioncomply-api — the API server (port 8080).
  • arioncomply-chroma — vector store (port 8000).
  • arioncomply-sweep.timer — scheduled background work (notification delivery, fact recompute, cite verification overdue, freshness expiry, retention cleanup).

14. What's next

Near-term

  • NIS 2 curation. The sector-vocabulary + region-column infrastructure is in place. Curating the NIS 2 controls and cross-framework bridges to ISO 27001 is the remaining work.
  • Customer data intake at scale. The first PoC customer is now provisioned and ready for real evidence. The intake pipeline is proven on 40+ Arion demo documents; scaling to a working customer will surface edge cases in text extraction, workbook shapes, and cited-source URL patterns.
  • Regional privacy regulations. LGPD (Brazil), CCPA (US-state), APPI (Japan), PIPEDA (Canada) — each is a multi-day curator arc that hangs off the existing region-scoping infrastructure without schema changes to the engine layer.

Structural bets

  • Sector-specific overlays. Once NIS 2 is curated, the same infrastructure supports HIPAA (health), PCI-DSS (payments), sector-specific supervisory guidance. Each becomes an obligation that composes with the tenant's ISO 27001 or ISO 27701 program.
  • Federated evidence. The cited-mode model can extend to automated read-only fetch of cited sources (with tenant-controlled credentials): the tenant still attests, but the system offers to show them what changed since last verification.
  • External-API expansion. Webhook subscriptions, write-side notifications and posture, key rotation, async SDK. Currently read-side is complete plus /query and evidence write; the write-side surfaces are the remaining API layer.
  • Superuser erasure-with-provenance flow. The deletion_log infrastructure and rag/deletion_service.py already exist — soft-delete, restore, anonymise, and handle_erasure_request() methods are implemented. What's missing is the workflow layer: a superuser-scoped API endpoint, a Profile UI to trigger it with reason capture, and (for higher-stakes deployments) a two-person approval rule. The first real GDPR Art.17 request or customer offboarding will trigger this arc — and because the plumbing is ready, the arc is "wire up + test" rather than "design from scratch".
